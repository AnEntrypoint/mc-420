Granulator (src/dsp/sampler/sampler.h) investigation history, drained from AGENTS.md during compaction.

Direct-dial addition history: before this work the whole LofiFx granulator knob bank (knobs 1-6) was 6 named-patch blend weights with knob7 unused -- every granulator parameter was only reachable by guessing which blend of fixed presets landed near what was wanted, never a direct 'turn this knob, hear that change' control. This was a real, identified contributor to user reports of the granulator being 'not easy to understand' and, since averaging 7 parameters across patches washes out any single patch's character, 'boring'. Current fix: kGranPatchCount reduced from 6 to 4 (dropping the two patches most redundant with the new direct dials), freeing knobs 5-7 for direct dials that override the corresponding patch-blended field only once actually touched (m_lofiFxKnobTouched[5..7]) -- purely additive over the prior patch-only behavior.

Grain-pool exhaustion measurement: MAX_GRAINS=48 shared across all 16 voices, while Density (knob 6) reaches 200Hz and the patch table carries a 200ms grain length -- 200ms x 200Hz demands ~42 concurrent grains for ONE voice, measured. A second voice then stole grains mid-window, a real discontinuity not a soft degrade: measured 735 mid-life thefts at 2 voices, 6350 at 16. Across the full production-reachable knob space (grainMs 14-200 x 2-200Hz x 1-16 voices, 336 configs) the shipped code stole in 68 configs for 34,726 total thefts. Measured after the per-voice budget fix: 0 thefts in all 336 configs and 0 across a 200-config randomized sweep of the wider setGrainPatch clamp range. On a 2-voice 220Hz sine case, broadband click energy above 8kHz dropped from -54.9dB to -88.6dB.

Gain-compensation bug this exposed: m_grainGainComp divided by the REQUESTED overlap while far fewer grains actually sounded, so adding notes faded the granulator out -- measured -16.67dBFS at 4 voices, -33.47 at 8, -48.34 at 16, a 43dB collapse with no indication why. Fixed by tracking the budgeted spawn period instead, giving -4.98/-5.02/-5.13dBFS, flat within 0.5dB of the 1-voice level. This removed what had been acting as an accidental -43dB limiter: with 16 held keys the summed output already exceeds int16 full scale with the granulator OFF, so downstream headroom now genuinely matters at the extreme.

Why MAX_GRAINS was deliberately NOT raised: measured worst case ~11.9ns per grain-sample on x86 -O3, so 48 grains is ~37-49us of the 1333us block budget (2.8-3.7%); growing the pool 4x multiplies that worst case by 4, and an x86 number cannot clear an aarch64 RT path. The measurement also shows the pool is not the binding constraint: one voice never demands more than 42 even at the extreme, and the fair shares (24/16/12/6/3 at 2/3/4/8/16 voices) match or beat what contention was already delivering (40/26/17/8/4) -- with zero thefts and correct level now. Raising it later is a one-line change since everything scales off it.

Grain pitch quantization measurement (the Clouds trick): continuous spray at 700 cents gives 601 distinct pitches spanning -676..+664 cents; the four discrete interval sets (Octaves/Fifths/Minor Triad/Minor Pentatonic) give exactly 3/5/5/7. Rendered FFT on a 220Hz source: set members sit within ~6dB of the fundamental and non-members at the -45..-51dB floor, where continuous mode is a smear with no discrete structure.

Additivity verification: over a 200-config randomized sweep at default quantize, 108 configs are byte-identical to the shipped header and 90 differ -- and in every differing case the baseline was actively stealing grains, i.e. output is byte-identical everywhere except where total grain demand exceeds the shared pool (exactly the failure the budget fix addresses). The interval-quantize feature itself contributes zero delta at its default (no RNG consumed, rate multiplied by exactly 1.0 on that path).

Grain timing jitter fix history: _renderGranularVoice fired grains at an exactly regular period derived from grainRateHz/velocity with zero inter-grain timing variation (only position/pitch had randomization) -- a perfectly regular grain clock reads as mechanical/synthetic regardless of how much position/pitch jitter is layered on top, a second independent 'boring' contributor alongside the patch-blend-only control surface. Fixed with a fixed internal kGrainTimingJitterAmt=0.15 (+/-15%) random deviation to each grain's own inter-onset interval, matching Resonode's own positionDriftAmt/stretchJitterAmt internal-constant convention. Verified via a standalone C++ harness linking sampler.h directly.

Default-patch reorder history: kGranPatches[0] (the fallback before any patch-weight knob is touched) used to be the 200ms/8Hz/no-spray/no-jitter 'soft pad' corner, the least obviously-granular-sounding patch available -- so a first-time press of the LofiFx button produced the least characterful texture in the whole table, a real contributor to a first impression of 'boring'. Reordered so index 0 is the 90ms/35Hz/25-cent-spray/35ms-jitter patch instead, with zero other behavior change (same 4 patches, same blend math, only array order/fallback identity moved).
