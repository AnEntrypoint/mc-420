Moving a numerator coefficient out of a Faust fi.tf2 biquad into a post-multiply is NOT an identity unless that coefficient is constant, and in resonode_synth.dsp it never is. The trap: b0 = sqrt(K(1-r^2)) and modePeakGain = sqrt(K(1+r)/(1-r)) genuinely cancel to exactly (1-r) -- verified 7.4e-13 over the real clamped range r in [0.96402, 0.99998], and r>=1 is structurally unreachable because modeInvT60 is max/min clamped. So the algebra tempts you to run the resonator unscaled and multiply by (1-r), removing 96 sqrt calls per sample. Why it is wrong: b0 derives from poleRadius, hence from decayTime and damping, both morphGlide-d and therefore moving at AUDIO RATE on every knob sweep. In a transposed-direct-form-II biquad the numerator taps feed the state registers, so a coefficient that changes between samples is applied to different state in the two forms. Measured divergence 8.4 percent of peak while a pole glides against 3.6e-12 percent at rest, and it persists far past the 15ms glide because it decays through the resonator multi-second tail. There is no rescue: keeping b0 inside and rewriting only the divisor gives (1-r)/b0 = sqrt((1-r)/(K(1+r))), so the square-root count returns to two and the win is gone. The win and the correctness are mutually exclusive. The general rule this teaches: verifying a DSP refactor with static parameters proves nothing about a parameter that glides. Always re-verify with the coefficient in motion. The same review also rejected de-gliding the five shape weights, because they feed poleRadius as well as freqMode, so a slider step moves a1 and a2 in one sample on a filter holding up to 8.5s of stored energy. What IS safe by contrast: the Chebyshev position sine, sin(pi*pos*(i+1)) = sin(pi*pos)*U_i(cos(pi*pos)), because it is a plain output MULTIPLIER in a product chain and never a filter coefficient. sin 64 to 4, float32 error -107 dBFS.
