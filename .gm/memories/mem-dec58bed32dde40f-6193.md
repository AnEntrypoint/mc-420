Free-transpose engine (soladSnacOctaver.h/EngineSoladSnac, the mono -12 pedal-ride effect) investigation history, drained from AGENTS.md during compaction.

Crossfade-length-division discovery: m_xfadeLen is set from one input period but counted down in OUTPUT samples, while readers advance m_scale input samples per output sample. Left undivided, readers travel per*scale during the fade and drift apart by (scale-1) periods before it ends, so the equal-gain fade ends up summing two uncorrelated reads. This was a real, reported microclick: measured at 40x the signal's own median slew rate, appearing at +10 semitones (exactly where drift crosses three quarters of a period) and worsening monotonically to +14, clean at +9 and below, concentrated at low input pitches where the period is long. Instrumenting the engine put 30 of 30 clicks adjacent to a splice and none away from one. Dividing the crossfade length by the pitch ratio keeps reader travel at one period regardless of shift: clicks go to zero across 112 synthetic cases and the whole 70-200Hz boundary sweep. The divisor is clamped to never go below 1.0 so downshift/unity are bit-identical over 40 cases -- the fix only engages on upshifts. Onset latency unchanged at 1.35-1.40ms across every pitch/shift.

Splice-parameter sweep, all measured against the real instrument corpus using degradation ratio (output roughness / that file's own input roughness -- chosen because it doesn't punish breathy sources for being breathy: vocal files score 0.34-0.45 roughness at the INPUT and the engine only adds ~7%, whereas violin input is 0.017 and the shipped engine took it to 0.106). Mean degradation over 16 instruments fell from 2.82 to 2.21 with the crossfade-division fix, worst cases improving most: violin low 6.10->3.03, violin high 4.78->2.83, cello 4.06->2.70. Four further knobs were swept and every one is already at its optimum -- do not re-tune without new evidence: m_respliceFrac 1.0 is best (2.215) vs 1.5 (3.176) and 2.0 (2.996), letting drift accumulate before correcting is clearly worse; SINC_TAPS 16 vs 32 is a dead heat (2.215 vs 2.216), doubling the kernel doubles interpolation cost for nothing since the residual is splice quality not interpolation quality; splice candidate search width +-3 is best (2.215) vs +-6 (2.223) and +-10 (2.249), a wider search finds candidates matching on value but further from the ideal period; m_transientHold at 2 periods is neutral (2.215/2.217/2.216 for 2/1/0 periods) so kept for double-played-attack protection; slope-match weight of 80.0 is flat across 20-400 (2.213-2.215), not worth churning; crossfade length multiplier is best at exactly one period (1.0 gives 2.215 vs 0.5=2.220, 0.75=2.218, 1.5=2.665, 2.0=2.821), longer fades are much worse; crossfade shape stays linear, a smoothstep ramp measures marginally worse (2.219 vs 2.215) despite having zero slope at both ends.

The crossfade-division change trades slightly against barely-pitched material, and that trade is the right way round: on the instrument corpus it's a clear win (2.821->2.215), but on speech/noise wavs at the corpus root it splits -- dingle improves 3.58->3.32 while cleetus regresses 1.80->2.03. Measuring voiced fraction explains it: cleetus is only 19% voiced vs dingle's 31% and machine1's 69%; on unpitched content there's no true period so a shorter fade gives the reader less averaging over noise. This engine is a pitch-lock for pitched playing so the pitched case is the one that matters; the regression is confined to material the effect isn't for.

SNAC period systematic bias measurement (not acted on): median error -4.2c at 82Hz, +4.3c at 110Hz, +13.8c at 261Hz, -17.0c at 587Hz, -42.7c at 880Hz, spread only ~0.1c (bias not jitter). Cause: the parabolic interpolator on a narrowing autocorrelation peak -- at 880Hz a period is 54.5 samples so the peak spans ~7 samples and the 1-sample lag grid is 15% of the peak width. Deliberately left alone: converted to splice phase error it's under 3 degrees across the whole musical range (0.9 degrees at 82-110Hz, 2.9 at 261Hz), and triggerSpliceByPeriod's value+slope candidate search already corrects for it; only the 880Hz corner reaches 8.8 degrees, above this effect's useful range.

MIN_PERIOD=48 (1000Hz tracking ceiling) rejection: raising to 32 was measured and rejected -- it genuinely raises the ceiling but real wet-output pitch accuracy got worse, since short-period candidates jitter between adjacent SNAC values sweep-to-sweep, injecting real phase error into splice cadence that a stable-but-technically-wrong subharmonic lock never had.

Formant control measurement: real, strong, measured on real audio -- positive-side brightening is large and clean; negative side is flatter/more non-monotonic in short windows (an inherent asymmetry in the factor=powf(2,d) mapping, not an engine defect). A real monotonic RMS loudness cost accompanies brightening; a make-up-gain fix was measured (grain-path RMS ~57-73% of clean-reader RMS) but deliberately not implemented -- needs by-ear tuning on real hardware, not a blind constant.

DUBFX_POLY_BS block-size reduction (64->16) history: this is the SAME finding documented in the multitranspose.dsp/pitch_poly_ffi.h section -- see multitranspose-investigation-history memory and AGENTS.md's current LpcFormantShifter::beginBlock note for the full history including the beginBlock cadence regression this change caused and its fix. The mono DUBFX_BS stays 64 unchanged: its latency is dominated by downshift reader geometry not buffering, so the same block-size change buys it nothing.

Historical note on shared splice implementation: this section used to contrast EngineSoladSnac against multitranspose.dsp's own, since-removed, xpose() two-tap delay shifter as 'structurally different splice algorithms'. That's no longer true -- multitranspose.dsp's 6 voices now each run this exact same EngineSoladSnac class, so there is only one splice/PSOLA implementation in the codebase, shared by both the mono and polyphonic engines. Measured spectral purity (0.99-1.00 THD on a clean sine sweep up to 5000Hz, no degradation near 700Hz) applies to this shared class in general.
